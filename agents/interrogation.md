# Interrogation

Static questionnaires are useful as a baseline. The stronger system generates new questions from contradictions in the enterprise model.

## Founders

Ask about:

- why the company was created
- original assumptions
- assumptions that proved wrong
- what almost killed the company
- decisions that still remain centralised
- decisions they refuse to delegate
- who can change their mind
- what information they personally verify
- which old process exists because of history
- which technology investments actually changed the company
- what they think is the biggest problem

## Leadership

Interview executives independently.

Ask:

- what are the top priorities?
- what prevents them?
- which metric do you distrust?
- where does information arrive too late?
- what decision takes absurdly long?
- what problem does everyone know about but nobody owns?
- who or what can quietly stop a major initiative?

Differences between leadership answers are themselves evidence.

## Employees

Frontline reality often differs from formal process.

Ask:

- what part of the job is unnecessarily difficult?
- what requires repeated copying and pasting?
- which information is hardest to find?
- what official process do people work around?
- which spreadsheet is secretly critical?
- what do managers misunderstand about the work?
- what would break if one person disappeared?
- what should be automated?
- what should absolutely not be automated?

## Dynamic questions

Example:

```text
CEO: "Decisions are decentralised."

Operations: "Everything important still requires CEO approval."

System evidence: most material contracts require CEO approval.

NEXT QUESTION:
"You describe the company as decentralised, but most material contracts still require your approval. Why?"
```

The purpose of interrogation is not to trap people. It is to improve the enterprise model.

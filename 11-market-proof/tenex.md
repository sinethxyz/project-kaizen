# Tenex

## What Tenex currently says

Tenex positions itself as an **AI transformation partner** and says it helps enterprises move from **AI-absent to AI-native**.

Its transformation model spans strategy, product, process, and people. Its audits are explicitly designed to surface compelling AI use cases, quantify ROI, and produce an AI adoption roadmap.

Tenex also uses engineering pods and Forward Deployed Engineers, so the operator/implementation-heavy model is not unique to Project Kaizen.

Its 2026 careers page is heavily AI-centred, including Applied AI engineering, Forward Deployed Engineer roles, and multiple AI Strategist roles across data, enablement, process, and product.

## Fair comparison

```text
TENEX
enterprise
↓
identify high-value AI opportunities
↓
AI transformation / AI-native destination
```

versus the Project Kaizen hypothesis:

```text
PROJECT KAIZEN
enterprise
↓
neutral diagnosis
↓
identify actual constraint or opportunity
↓
select intervention
↓
AI where justified
```

The critique is **not** that Tenex does not solve real problems. It clearly addresses process, people, engineering, adoption, and ROI.

The narrower claim is that its company-level category definition starts with AI as the destination. Project Kaizen asks whether the prior question — `what should this enterprise change at all?` — creates a more durable decision layer.

## What Tenex validates

- buyers want AI transformation help
- embedded operators/FDEs are commercially credible
- strategy without implementation is insufficient
- people/change management matter
- business outcomes and ROI matter

## What must be tested

- do customers value technology-neutral diagnosis enough to pay for it?
- does a broader answer space improve outcomes or merely make positioning vague?
- can Project Kaizen preserve execution speed while refusing a predetermined technology destination?

# Definition of Better

Project Kaizen needs a stable answer to a simple question:

> **What does it mean for an enterprise to become better?**

The answer should remain broader than any one technology.

Persist should evaluate interventions against five durable qualities.

## 1. Truth

Does the organisation understand reality more accurately?

Examples:
- cleaner data;
- fewer conflicting metrics;
- clearer customer reality;
- better visibility into operations;
- fewer assumptions masquerading as facts.

## 2. Coherence

Do people, processes, systems, incentives, and information fit together better?

Examples:
- fewer contradictory workflows;
- clearer ownership;
- systems sharing the same definitions;
- incentives aligned with desired behaviour.

## 3. Decision quality

Can the enterprise convert information into better decisions?

Examples:
- faster decisions where speed matters;
- more deliberate decisions where risk matters;
- better evidence;
- fewer unnecessary escalations;
- clearer accountability.

## 4. Execution

Can decisions become reliable action?

Examples:
- lower cycle time;
- fewer handoff failures;
- less rework;
- higher operational reliability;
- clearer ownership of outcomes.

## 5. Adaptability

Can the enterprise change when the environment changes?

Examples:
- lower switching costs;
- modular systems;
- transferable capabilities;
- shorter learning loops;
- less dependence on one vendor, person, process, or technological wave.

## Canonical test

Every meaningful intervention should be able to answer:

```text
Did truth improve?
Did coherence improve?
Did decision quality improve?
Did execution improve?
Did adaptability improve?
```

Not every intervention must improve all five.

But if none improve, Persist should question why it is intervening at all.

> **Technology adoption is not the outcome. A better enterprise is the outcome.**

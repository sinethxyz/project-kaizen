# The Enterprise Is the Centre of Gravity

Most AI transformation thinking places AI at the centre and asks how the enterprise should reorganise around it.

Project Kaizen reverses the direction:

> **The enterprise is the centre of gravity. Technology orbits the enterprise.**

AI, cloud, ERP, SaaS, automation, robotics, and whatever comes next are capabilities whose relevance depends on the enterprise and its environment.

## Persist mission

From Sineth's point of view, Persist's mission should be:

> **Understand enterprises deeply enough to know what any new technology actually means for them, and how to debug or close the gaps inside the enterprise.**

The first half is about interpretation: understanding what a technological change actually changes for a specific enterprise.

The second half is about intervention: finding the gap between how the enterprise works today and how it needs to work, then deciding what should be fixed, redesigned, connected, removed, automated, or left alone.

```text
UNDERSTAND THE ENTERPRISE
↓
UNDERSTAND THE CHANGE
↓
IDENTIFY THE GAP
↓
DEBUG THE SYSTEM
↓
CLOSE THE GAP
↓
MEASURE WHAT CHANGED
↓
LEARN
↺
```

"Debug" is intentionally broader than software. An enterprise can contain bugs in data, processes, incentives, ownership, coordination, systems, decision making, information flow, or organisational design.

The objective is not to deploy more technology. The objective is to make the enterprise work better.

The durable capability is therefore:

> **Understand enterprises deeply enough to know what any new technology actually means for them.**

This does not make technology expertise less important. It changes its role. Technology expertise serves enterprise understanding rather than replacing it.

A useful sequence is:

```text
ENTERPRISE
↓
What creates value?
↓
How does the system actually work?
↓
What changed around it?
↓
Where is it misaligned?
↓
What should change?
↓
Which capability or technology helps?
```

AI is the current wave. The enterprise is the enduring object.

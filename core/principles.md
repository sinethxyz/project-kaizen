# Principles

## Order of reasoning

> **Business first. Systems second. Technology third. AI somewhere much later.**

AI may be fourth, tenth, or absent.

## Problem before capability

> **The problem should determine the capability. The capability should not determine the problem.**

A provider that begins with a predetermined solution space is naturally biased toward finding problems that fit it.

## Hiring rule

> **Do not let your payroll define your diagnosis.**

Permanent hiring should be strongest around enterprise understanding, diagnosis, orchestration, client trust, quality control, measurement, and learning.

Specialist depth can be internalised when repeated demand and strategic importance justify it.

## Minimum sufficient intervention

> **Understand deeply. Change only what needs changing.**

Prefer the smallest intervention likely to produce the required outcome. Expand only when evidence justifies expansion.

The best recommendation may be to do nothing.

## Definition of better

Technology adoption is not the outcome.

A useful intervention should improve one or more of:

1. **Truth** — the organisation understands reality more accurately.
2. **Coherence** — people, data, systems, ownership, and processes agree more often.
3. **Decision quality** — information becomes better decisions.
4. **Execution** — decisions become actions with less friction.
5. **Adaptability** — the enterprise can respond to change more effectively.

## Decision doctrine

> **AI proposes. Evidence constrains. Humans decide. Outcomes judge.**

AI should help reason, challenge, and search. It should not become an unaccountable authority over consequential enterprise decisions.

## Epistemic rule

> **The model is not the truth. The model is a machine for finding the truth.**

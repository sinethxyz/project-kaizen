# Enterprise Model

An enterprise is not simply an org chart plus software.

It is a living system through which people, information, capital, decisions, incentives, technology, and history interact to create outcomes.

A useful model includes:

```text
STRUCTURE
SYSTEMS
DATA
ECONOMICS
PROCESSES
PEOPLE
INCENTIVES
POWER
HISTORY
BEHAVIOUR
MARKET
TECHNOLOGY
```

## Formal versus real enterprise

The formal enterprise includes org charts, policies, stated processes, systems of record, KPIs, roles, permissions, and contracts.

The real enterprise also includes who actually decides, informal trust networks, hidden spreadsheets, workarounds, information monopolies, veto power, political constraints, and habits accumulated over time.

Both matter.

## Human system

Enterprises are human systems expressed through machines.

Trust, fear, status, identity, morale, loyalty, resistance, risk tolerance, and communication affect whether technically correct interventions actually work.

Do not infer inner motives as facts. Describe observable behaviour and test explanations against evidence.

## Power system

Map who can approve, veto, delay, influence, control budgets, control information, mobilise people, or protect the status quo.

The formal hierarchy is not always the power topology.

## Historical system

Today's enterprise is path dependent.

Founding assumptions, acquisitions, crises, regulation, early technical choices, founder habits, management layers, and old incentives can remain embedded long after their original reason disappears.

## Game theoretic system

Actors can have different incentives:

- CEO wants strategic movement
- CFO wants predictable economics
- CTO wants technical integrity
- department head protects control or headcount
- employees may resist monitoring or redundancy
- vendors want expansion
- operators want measurable outcomes

The technically optimal intervention may not be organisationally executable.

## Time matters

Model the enterprise through time, not only as a snapshot.

```text
FOUNDING
↓
EARLY CUSTOMERS
↓
HIRING
↓
SYSTEMS
↓
GROWTH
↓
ACQUISITIONS / REGULATION / CRISES
↓
TODAY
```

Understanding how the system formed often explains why it resists change now.
